# Expressions (meaning written here; not dictionary entries)
Table for two, please. | Asking the host for a table for two people when you arrive.
Do you have a reservation? | The host asking whether you booked a table in advance.
We have a reservation under Smith. | Telling the host the name the table was booked under.
How long is the wait? | Asking how long until a table is free.
Can we sit outside? | Asking for a table on the patio or outdoor area.
Are you ready to order? | The server asking if you have decided what to eat.
Can I get you started with anything to drink? | The server's usual first question: what you'd like to drink while you look at the menu.
I need a few more minutes. | Politely telling the server you haven't decided yet.
What do you recommend? | Asking the server for a suggestion from the menu.
What's good here? | Casual way to ask which dishes the restaurant does best.
I'll have the salmon. | The usual way to order a dish: "I'll have…" (also "Can I get…").
Can I get the burger, medium rare? | Ordering a dish and saying how you want it cooked; "Can I get…" is normal and polite in the US.
I'll go with the special. | Choosing the dish of the day; "go with" means choose.
Could I get the dressing on the side? | Asking for the sauce or dressing served separately, not on the food.
Can I get a salad instead of fries? | Asking to swap the side dish that comes with your meal; "sub" is the casual verb: "Can I sub a salad for the fries?"
Does that come with anything? | Asking what side dishes are included with a main dish.
Is this dish spicy? | Asking how hot the food is.
I'm allergic to nuts. | Telling the server about a food allergy so the kitchen can avoid it.
Just water for now, thanks. | Ordering only water while you decide on other drinks.
Could we get some more napkins? | Asking for an extra item; "Could we get…" is the polite form.
How is everything? | The server checking that you're happy with the meal; "Great, thanks" is the usual reply.
Everything's great, thanks. | The usual friendly reply when the server checks on you.
This isn't what I ordered. | Politely saying the server brought the wrong dish.
Could I get a box for this? | Asking for a container to take your leftovers home.
Can we get the check, please? | Asking for the bill at the end of the meal (Americans say "check").
Could we split the check? | Asking for separate bills so each person pays for their own food.
Can we put it all on one card? | Asking to pay the whole bill with one credit card.
It's on me. | Saying you will pay for everyone; also "My treat."
Let's go Dutch. | Suggesting that each person pays for their own meal.
Keep the change. | Telling the server to keep the leftover money as part of the tip.
For here or to go? | A counter worker asking whether you'll eat in the restaurant or take the food away.
I'm just grabbing something quick. | Saying you want a fast, simple meal.
Can I get a refill? | Asking for your drink to be filled again, often free in the US.
We're still working on it. | Telling the server you haven't finished eating, so don't take the plates yet.
I'm stuffed. | Casual way to say you've eaten so much you are completely full.
My compliments to the chef. | Formal way of saying the food was excellent.
