# Expressions
The sink is clogged. | Saying water won't drain from the sink.
The faucet is leaking. | Saying water is dripping from the faucet.
The power went out. | Saying the electricity stopped working.
The AC isn't working. | Saying the air conditioning is broken.
Can you send someone to take a look? | Asking a landlord or company to send a repair person.
When is rent due? | Asking the date rent must be paid.
Will I get my security deposit back? | Asking whether the landlord will return your deposit when you move out; by law it comes back minus any damage beyond normal wear and tear.
I'd like to make an appointment. | Asking to schedule a visit with a doctor or service.
Do you take my insurance? | Asking whether the clinic accepts your health insurance plan.
Are you accepting new patients? | Asking whether a doctor can take you as a regular patient.
I'm not feeling well. | Saying you feel sick.
I think I'm coming down with something. | Saying you feel the first signs of an illness.
I've had a fever since yesterday. | Describing how long you have had a symptom.
It hurts when I swallow. | Describing pain in your throat.
I feel lightheaded. | Saying you feel dizzy, as if you might faint.
My back is killing me. | Casual: my back hurts a lot.
I need to refill my prescription. | Asking for more of a medicine your doctor prescribed.
Are there any side effects? | Asking about unwanted effects of a medicine.
Take two tablets twice a day. | Typical instructions for how to take a medicine.
Take it with food. | Instructions to swallow a medicine during or after a meal.
How long is the wait? | Asking how long until you will be seen.
What's the copay? | Asking how much you pay at the visit under your insurance.
I'm calling in sick today. | Telling your employer you can't work because you are ill.
Feel better soon! | A kind wish to someone who is sick.
Bless you. | What Americans say when someone sneezes.
Gesundheit. | Another common thing Americans say when someone sneezes.
