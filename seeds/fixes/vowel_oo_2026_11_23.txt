# Tips that mix up the long oo of food and the short oo of book.
stooped | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Say it as one syllable and keep the p-t ending clean. The vowel is the same long 'oo' as in 'stoop' and 'food', not the short vowel of 'put' or 'book'.
stooping | nuance.pronunciation.tip | The stress falls on the first syllable, and the vowel is the long oo of 'food' and 'stoop', not the short oo of 'book'. Keep the final ng clean and do not add a hard g.
bridegroom | nuance.pronunciation.tip | The main stress falls on BRIDE, with a lighter secondary stress on groom. Do not say 'bridge-groom'; the d is clear and groom has the long oo of 'food'.
game-room | nuance.pronunciation.tip | This is a compound noun, so Americans stress the first word: GAME room, not game ROOM. The second word is lighter but keeps the long oo of 'boom'; some speakers use the shorter vowel of 'book'.
locker-room | nuance.pronunciation.tip | In this compound, the strongest stress is on LOCK, with 'room' lighter. The 'r' in 'locker' is pronounced, and 'room' usually has the long 'oo' of 'boom'; some speakers use the shorter vowel of 'book'.
sunroom | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress SUN and make the second syllable 'room,' rhyming with 'broom,' with the long oo of 'food'. Some speakers use the shorter vowel of 'book'.
classroom | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress 'class' with the short a of 'cat'. The second part is usually 'room' with the long oo of 'boom'; some speakers use the shorter vowel of 'book'. Both are common.
permutation-group | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the TAY in permutation and the GROOP in group. Americans say 'group' with a clear r and the long oo of 'food', and the two words should be joined with light stress on the first word.
poustinia | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the third syllable, NEE. The word is borrowed from Russian; the first vowel is the oo of 'food', and the final -ia is weakly pronounced as 'uh'.
unenthusiastic | nuance.pronunciation.tip | The main stress falls on AS, the syllable of 'enthusiasm' that carries the stress. The en- is light, the th is followed by the oo of 'food' (no y sound in most American speech), and the final -ic is 'ik'.
perdure | nuance.pronunciation.tip | The stress falls on the second syllable, DUR, and the first syllable is a light, r-colored 'per'. DUR rhymes with 'tour' and 'sure': no y sound after the d.
wormwood | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the first syllable, with the r-colored 'ur' vowel. The second syllable is 'wood', with the short oo of 'good' and 'would', not the long oo of 'food'.
would-be | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on WOULD, and 'be' is a weak 'bee' sound with a reduced vowel. Do not stress 'be'. 'Would' sounds exactly like 'wood', with the short oo of 'book', not the long oo of 'food'.
repairwoman | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the first syllable of 'repair' on 'PAIR', with a weaker stress on 'wom'. The 'o' in 'woman' is the short oo of 'wood' and 'book', not the long oo of 'food'.
pull-up-a-chair | nuance.pronunciation.tip | The main stress falls on 'chair', and 'a' is a reduced schwa. Link the phrase so it sounds like 'puul-up-uh-CHAIR', with the short oo of 'book' in 'pull' (not 'pool'), and keep the American r in 'chair'.
luke | nuance.pronunciation.tip | It rhymes with 'duke' and 'nuke', with the long oo of 'food' and no y sound. It does not sound like 'look'. It is the first half of lukewarm.
luke-the-evangelist | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Luke is one syllable that rhymes with 'duke', with the long oo of 'food'. It does not sound like 'look'. Stress VAN in Evangelist.
gospel-of-luke | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on GAH and LUKE, and 'of' reduces to 'uv'. Luke rhymes with 'duke', with the long oo of 'food', not the short vowel of 'book' or 'look'.
lawsuit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on LAW, and the second syllable is 'suit', with the long oo of 'food', not 'syoot.' The y sound given in some dictionaries is not common in General American here.
pursuit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the second syllable, SUIT, with the long oo of 'food' and no y sound. Do not stress the first syllable, as in PUR-suit.
hot-pursuit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress SUIT: "hot pur-SUIT." The vowel is the long oo of "food," with no "yoo."

jumpsuit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress 'jump'; 'suit' has a lighter stress and rhymes with 'boot', with a long oo.
strong-suit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on SUIT, and the vowel is the oo of 'boot', not 'sweet' or 'sute'. The final t is light and may be almost silent before a pause.
suit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | General American has no y sound after s, so 'suit' rhymes with 'boot', not 'syoot.' The vowel is a long oo, as in 'food.'
swimsuit | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the first syllable, SWIM. The second part, suit, rhymes with 'boot'; most Americans say it with no y sound.
hirsute | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on the second syllable, which rhymes with 'suit' and 'boot', and the first syllable is a rhotic 'er' sound. Learners often say 'HIR-sute' with stress on the first syllable.
hirsuteness | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress falls on the middle syllable, which rhymes with 'suit', not on the first. The ending -ness is reduced to 'nus', and the first syllable is a soft, r-colored 'hur'.
sounds-good | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Good is pronounced with a short 'oo' vowel, like 'book', not the long 'food' vowel. Sounds has a final z sound, so keep it voiced.
stateswoman | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress the first syllable. 'Woman' is pronounced 'WUUM-uhn' in American speech, with the short oo of 'book', not 'WAH-man'.
businesswoman | nuance.pronunciation.tip | Stress 'BIZ'. 'Business' has two syllables, 'BIZ-nus', with no 'i' sound in the middle, and 'woman' is 'WUUM-un', with the short oo of 'book'.
